## 2026-09-18: Diagnose S4 boundary-F1 stagnation as a paragraph-axis granularity ceiling, not a scorer defect

- **Context:** PLAN.md's 2026-09-18 S4 session flagged an open question: boundary micro F1 stayed exactly 0.576 (tol0) / 0.727 (tol2) even though the per-boundary snap-collision fix moved 20 more boundaries from raw interpolation to phrase-snapped positions (6/32 -> 26/32) on the 5 multi-boundary predicted gold days. A per-boundary gold-vs-predicted comparison (reusing existing s4_paragraph_axis_predictions.jsonl and boundary_gold_paragraph_axis, no rerun) found the cause: gold's own boundary annotations repeat the identical paragraph_stream_index for multiple distinct K_e-1 cut points whenever the paragraph axis is coarser than the resolution count (e.g. 1626-02-28: 6 boundary slots collapse onto 1 distinct axis position, axis_count=8 for k_e=7). - **Decision:** Treat boundary_f1_tolerance0/tolerance2 as having hit a structural ceiling from paragraph-level axis granularity, not a defect in interpolation/snapping. Stop trying to move these two metrics via better interpolation or phrase-snapping; that effort is spent. Any further F1 gain requires finer-than-paragraph segmentation, which is the already-scoped line-level-segmentation track (state.json status: blocked, pending htr_classified_lines.csv date-coverage verification). S4's other metrics (predicted-day coverage, corpus baseline coverage, paragraph-axis entity coverage) are unaffected and remain open.
- **Reason:** compute_boundary_prf awards at most one hyp match per distinct ref value, and predicted boundary sequences are always strictly increasing (never repeat a position), so duplicate-valued gold slots impose a hard recall ceiling regardless of predictor quality: max reachable TP = count of distinct ref values, not len(ref). Verified on the 5 multi-boundary predicted days: tol0 TP (19) already reaches 79% of that ceiling (24 distinct values across 34 ref slots) and tol2 TP (24) reaches exactly 100% of it -- the snap-collision fix improved individual boundary position accuracy, but the metric was already saturated against the axis-imposed ceiling both before and after, which is why F1 did not move.

## 2026-09-20: Correction: the paragraph-granularity ceiling is rigorous only at tolerance 0

- **Context:** The 2026-09-18 entry closing boundary_f1_tolerance0/tolerance2 stated that compute_boundary_prf 'can award at most one hyp match per distinct ref value', giving 'max reachable TP = count of distinct ref values', and read the 5-day subset result 'tol2 TP (24) reaches exactly 100% of it' as evidence the metric was saturated. The S6 guide written 2026-09-20 initially repeated that framing. Probed it directly against evaluation_harness.compute_boundary_prf.
- **Decision:** The claim holds at tolerance 0 only. At tol=0 a hypothesis must equal a reference exactly, and since predictions are strictly increasing, at most one can claim each distinct ref value -- a hard bound. At tol>0 it is not a property of the metric: against ref=[7,7,7], a CLUSTERED hypothesis [5,6,7] scores TP=3 at tolerance 2, while a spread [3,7,11] scores TP=1. The bound appeared to hold at tol2 only because the interpolate-then-snap predictor emits one cut per paragraph by construction and so cannot cluster. Therefore tol2 was NOT provably saturated on 2026-09-18; there was headroom the ceiling framing concealed. Corrected in docs/steps/STEP_S6_anchor_chain_alignment.md section 1(a); the tol0 figures (280/352 = 79.5% paragraph coords, 320/352 = 90.9% char coords) are unaffected and remain rigorous.
- **Reason:** The overstated version would have mis-set S6's expectations twice over: it understates how much tol2 headroom the current predictor is leaving on the table, and it credits the metric with a limitation that actually belongs to the predictor family. Both readings still argue for character coordinates -- placing several cuts inside one paragraph is exactly what they enable -- so the S6 direction is unchanged, but the stated reason had to be made accurate before the track is executed against it.
